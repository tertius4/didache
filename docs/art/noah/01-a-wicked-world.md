# Noah lesson 1: A wicked world (Genesis 6:1–7)

Save the images to `static/media/bible-stories/noah/a-wicked-world/`, and the voice-overs to the same folder with the same names (`.mp3`).

**Arc of the lesson:** life spreads → sin spreads → God sees the heart → God is grieved and judgment is coming → but God is patient, and one light still burns. The mood moves from **Calm** through **Shadow** to **Judgment**, and ends on a small note of **Grace**.

No named characters appear in this lesson, so no character lines are needed. Every prompt below is complete: style block + scene + avoid block. Paste each one as a whole.

On both banks, small clusters of mud-brick houses with flat roofs, and more villages scattered over the rolling hills toward the horizon, thinning out with distance. Smoke rises from cooking fires. Fields of green and gold, small flocks of sheep, a few tiny people working the fields and walking the paths between villages. The left side feels older and more settled; the right side has new houses still being built. The overall feeling is life spreading over a young world.

The sky at the top edge is dusky violet. The mood is sober and sad, not frightening.

**Shows:** A link to lesson 2 (Noah), without showing Noah. Night has fallen. A dark town is spread across the lower part of the frame, with only a few dim lamps. On a low hill on the right, a little apart from the town, stands a single small mud-brick house with an olive tree beside it. One window glows warm, bright gold, the warmest point in the whole image, and it casts a soft pool of light onto the grass. Above, the storm clouds have parted slightly, and a few stars show through the gap. Quiet hope.

### Tips for this lesson

- Make image 1 first and use it as the **style reference** for images 2–5, so the valley, the houses and the paper texture stay consistent.
- Images 4 and 5 show the same valley, which makes the crossfade work well. If the generator supports it, use image 4 as an image reference for 5.
- Image 3 is the hardest. If the generator crowds it, keep only the coin-counter, the feast with the hungry child and the idol.
